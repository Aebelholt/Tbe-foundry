The Lone Weaver

One player, one Gamemaster, both of them you. Twenty macros, one hotbar row, and how a session actually runs. Plus the fifth of Foundry worth knowing.

The table, set once

Twenty minutes the first evening, never again.

  1. World on Call of Cthulhu 7eCreate the world on the CoC7 system. It provides the d100 sheet, tokens, and item plumbing; every rule that matters runs through the TBE macros instead.
  2. Run the three installersEach is one Script macro: paste, save, click. Solo pack first, then equipment, then bestiary. All three are safe to re-run when updated, and they report to the console (F12, lines starting TBE |) if anything objects.
  3. Build your characterMake a blank Character actor, select its token, run TBE: Build Character. It stocks the sheet with the TBE skill list and creates your TBE Sheet journal for everything CoC7 has no field for. Then do Chapter 7 chargen by typing values in. Right-click the actor and choose Assign as Character so the macros find you without a token selected.
  4. Arm yourselfDrag weapons from the TBE Weapons folder onto your sheet. TBE: Attack reads skill, damage, and maneuver costs off the item, so a weapon on the sheet is a weapon that works.
  5. Lay the hotbarEverything lives on keys 1–0. The whole game is one row of your keyboard.

The scene is a stage, not a grid. TBE thinks in zones, not squares. A plain backdrop scene with three or four drawn rectangles labelled by zone quality — Open, Rough, the ford — carries a whole fight. Skip measured movement entirely; a token sitting inside a rectangle is the position record.

The loop

A session is this sequence, repeated until bedtime.

  1. Frame the sceneSay your expectation aloud or in the box: “I ride to the Death Knight's castle.” Run TBE: Subverted Scene. Expected, Less, More, or Unexpected — the macro rolls the follow-up words or the interrupting Event itself.
  2. Act toward a goalYour character does something that pursues what they want. If the outcome is uncertain, roll it: TBE: Skill Roll with a task modifier, or TBE: Attack if steel is out. If your skill makes success obvious, don't roll at all — take 1 SL and move.
  3. Ask when you don't knowAsk the Weave, with odds, phrased so yes creates a complication: not “is the chest safe?” but “is the chest trapped?”. Doubles fire a Random Event on top of the answer — the macro handles it, including the Empires List draw when the event calls for one.
  4. Interpret, first instinctThe two randomizer words plus context. If nothing comes in ten seconds, re-roll or discard; second-guessing kills solo games faster than any monster.
  5. Record what now mattersA new face → TBE: NPC rolls their name, look, aspect, agenda and reaction trigger, and files them on the Empires List for you. Something long begun → TBE: Clocks. Then TBE: Session Log for the two sentences that make this readable in a month.

Running your characters

Selection is who acts; targeting is who suffers. Click a token to select it — the macros roll with that actor's skills and weapons. Hover an enemy and press T to target it — that's who the attack resolves against. This one pair of habits is most of “controlling actors” in Foundry.

Hirelings and allies are just more Character actors: build them with TBE: Build Character at whatever skill base fits, park their tokens beside yours, and select whichever one is acting. The book's advice stands — don't go into the dungeon alone. Enemy turns use the same tool in reverse: select the creature's token, target your PC, run TBE: Attack. One macro plays both sides.

A fight, start to finish

  1. Set the stageDrag the creature from TBE Bestiary onto the scene. Describe the zones like a GM would — elevation, mud, fire, cover. Interesting ground is what makes maneuvers worth choosing.
  2. Order of actionEnemies act on the static Initiative in their bio; you roll d10 + your Initiative once. Higher first, then alternate. The combat tracker is optional overhead — for one or two combatants a side, just remember who's fast.
  3. Your exchangeSelect your PC, T the foe, TBE: Attack. Pick the weapon — skill and damage come with it. Defence is pre-picked from the foe's Dodge or best parry skill. One dialog, one Strike.
  4. Win the clash, pick a maneuverThe popup lists every Combat Maneuver with that weapon's own costs, greyed where your rolled SLs fall short. The rule the macro enforces so you don't have to: maneuvers gate on rolled SLs and are never spent; damage uses DoS. A critical adds +3 SL and unlocks two picks.
  5. The wound resolves itselfHit location from the dice, armour and shield AP from the creature's grid, Circumvent Shield and Pierce Armor applied if chosen, Wound Die with Toughness, impairment called out, WP subtracted from HP. Trip and Shield Bash set Prone on the sheet; Unbalance and Lock leave a token icon.
  6. Their exchangeSelect the creature, target you, same macro. It writes the wound to your sheet exactly the way it writes one to a foe's — same per-location WP, same impairment count, same token icons. Nothing to copy into the journal by hand.
Shields are that strong. Damage + DoS must clear armour plus shield AP. A hand axe against a shielded bandit often needs Circumvent Shield or a fat DoS to draw blood. That's the book working as intended, not the macro failing you — and it's why Splitting the Shield exists.

Clocks are everything long

One mechanic, many skins: the extended roll.

TBE: Clocks keeps them: name a clock, set SLs required (3 easy · 6 standard · 9 difficult · 12+ major), an interval, and an optional limit. Each advance rolls a skill from the selected token — or takes SLs typed in directly, for helpers and half-progress rulings. Successes accumulate, a critical failure halves the total (rounding up), and hitting the limit closes the opportunity. Every clock persists in the TBE Clocks journal with a progress bar, so pop that journal out and pin it beside chat.

Travel leg

6 SL, interval a day, limit = days of rations. Roll Survival or Track; a failed day also triggers an event roll. The full Chapter 12 machine, one clock.

Social encounter

Need 3–6 SL of Persuade, Wit, Protocol in sequence; limit 4–5 exchanges as the Timer. Failure adds a complication; a critical failure is the door closing.

Crafting

Straight from the book's table — a Medium weapon is 12 SL of Craft: Practical, interval a day. The macro's crit-fail halving is the ruined-work rule.

Investigation

9 SL over the scene, every 3 SL banked reveals the next clue. Gradual outcomes without extra machinery.

After the blood

The part most VTT tools skip, and the part that actually kills you.

Twenty thousand simulated fights say the same thing: characters fall to Shock roughly four times as often as to the Death Threshold, and Shock comes from a second impairment in one location. That makes per-location wounds the state worth tracking, and TBE: Wounds & Recovery is where they live.

Attack writes wounds there automatically, location by location, with impairment counts, and offers the 3-Resolve refusal when Shock lands. Afterwards the same macro does the rest of Chapter 11: Stabilize the dying, Treat a wound (which sets its Recovery Bonus and can never be redone), remove an impairment, rouse someone from Shock, purge an infection, and run a night's Recovery roll per wound with the cleanliness modifier. Every Heal attempt burns the Medical Supply Die, as the book demands, and a critical failure degrades it automatically.

Untreated wounds set at -20. A wound left alone for an hour fixes its own Recovery Bonus at -20 and can never be treated afterwards. Treating in the field, badly, still beats not treating at all.

Every state that matters now shows on the token, not just in a chat card: each impaired location gets its own icon, Shock and Dying and Infected and Septic all get one too, and so do Unbalance/Lock/Disarm/Disadvantaged riders and a caster's own Restrained from a bad Weave Reaction — TBE: Attack, TBE: Wounds & Recovery, and TBE: Cast apply and clear these for you. TBE: Status Effects is the manual side of the same folder — mark Fatigue, Weary, Deprived, Sleepless, Blinded, Frostbite, Burning, Poisoned, or Feared when the fiction calls for one, since nothing rolls those automatically yet. The full list, book page references included, lives in the TBE: Status & Peril Reference journal the installer creates; TBE: Status also repaints every icon to match your actual wounds whenever you open it, so nothing stays stale if a step gets skipped.

The road and the pack

TBE: Journey Leg compresses the eight-step leg into the rolls one player actually makes: the Guide's Track roll for distance (half your Score, plus a hex per SL), the Quartermaster's Survival roll against your rations, the arrival Infection check for anyone carrying lethal wounds, the Scout's Perception roll for warning, then the Event itself with odds that shift by hex danger. Take the free Lone Wanderer Talent and the -40 for holding all three duties disappears.

TBE: Supply keeps Gear, Ammo, Medical and Rations on their d12 to d6 track. It runs itself where it matters: a bow shot rolls Ammo, a Heal roll rolls Medical, a night's rest and a failed forage roll Rations.

Magic that bites back

TBE: Cast takes the Bind roll, the control check, and the whole d20 Weave Reaction table. Declare the effect, name the shortfall in Total Cost you could not cover, then decide how much Resolve to spend mitigating before you know whether the target resists. Spend nothing and the Reaction rolls; a critical failure adds +5 to it. NPC casters use the Chapter 18 shortcut instead, where the ones die against the Strand decides control, and the macro reads the ones die back as the struck hit location for damaging spells.

Foundry, the fifth you need

Foundry is a database with a map on top. Five document types matter here, and nothing else does.

ThingWhat it isWhere
ActorA character sheet. The token is its playing piece; deleting a token never deletes the actor.Actors tab
ItemA document on an actor — skills and weapons are items. Drag to copy, edit via the row's button.Items tab, or on sheets
JournalPages of text. Your TBE Sheet, session log, clocks, and references live here. Pop out with the window icon.Journal tab

Play as the GM login. Solo has no secrets to keep, and GM permission is what lets the macros write wounds and conditions onto any sheet.

Making Foundry disappear

The aim is that a session touches the keyboard row, the T key, and the chat log — nothing else.

One key, whole game

When in doubt, key 1: the Solo Panel lists every tool. Muscle memory for 2, 4, and 8 comes free within a session.

Pin the paper

Pop out your TBE Sheet, the session log, and TBE Clocks as floating windows. Foundry remembers window positions per client.

Let chat be the record

Every macro posts a card — the chat log is the session transcript. Skim it before the next session instead of taking separate notes.

Physical dice feel

Dice So Nice rolls 3D dice for every macro roll. For solo immersion it earns its install more than any other module.

When something ghosts

Stale hotbar warnings or a sheet not refreshing: F5 first. Installer complaints: F12, read the TBE | lines, send them onward.

Update without fear

Re-running any installer updates in place: hotbar slots survive, and a filled-in Empires List is never overwritten.
