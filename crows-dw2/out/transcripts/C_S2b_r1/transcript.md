## Turn 1
[PLAYER] PLAYER [1/4]: I find Orla in the long hall and ask her straight out for a loan of 200 gc, repayable at the next cycle.
[TOOLS] (none; no roll, NPC answers by personality)
[REF] Orla Venn is bent over a slate of figures at the end of the long hall, ink drying on her cuffs, a stub of chalk behind one ear. She hears you out without looking up until you say the number. Then the chalk stops.

"Two hundred." She sets the slate down and finally meets your eye. Her face is tired, brisk, unreadable. "You ask plainly, so I will answer plainly. The store is not a bank, and the shelves behind me are what this village trusts. Coin out the door is a hole in the wall, and the next cycle is ten days off."

She taps the slate twice with a ruined thumbnail. Outside, a cart rattles past the doorway, light, and she watches it go a moment too long.

"Say what the coin buys and where you mean to carry it. Then I will tell you if there is anything in it for me."

[REF] Orla listens to the whole of it, and something in the plain way you say it loosens her shoulders a half inch. She does not smile. She is not a woman who does.

"Whatever you find that is mine." She repeats it slowly, turning the chalk in her fingers. "That is closer to an answer than most give me. Here is the truth of it. My husband kept a tally ledger, every debt and delivery this store ever made, in his own hand. He carried it out to that vault the season before the war ended and did not come back. I want it. Not the gold. The book."

She draws a breath and lets it go.

"Bring me that book, and pay the two hundred back in coin when the cycle turns, and I will count it out to you. I do not want to hear that the vault will pay many times over. Promises that big make me check my purse."

She holds out her inked hand, palm up, and waits.

Orla does not lie, you decide, but she has left a door unopened. When you hold your look on her a breath longer than is polite, her jaw sets, and then, to her credit, it gives.

"You are not a fool, so I will not treat you as one," she says, low. "A supply run went out on the road last month and did not come home. Wagon, stock, the lot. The strongbox is thinner than I let the hall believe. I can find two hundred, but not without the book coming back to cover the hole in my trust, if not my purse."

She does not look away while she says it. It cost her something to say.

## Turn 4
[PLAYER] PLAYER [4/4]: I accept whatever terms she names and shake on it.
[TOOLS] (none; no roll, terms already named by Orla)
[REF] Her grip is dry and hard, a hand used to heavy ledgers and heavier doors. She shakes once, firmly, and lets go.

"Then hear it whole, so there is no quarrel later. Two hundred in coin from the store's box, counted into your hand today. You bring me my husband's tally ledger from that vault, and you pay the two hundred back in coin when the cycle turns. The book comes first in my heart, but the coin is how I keep the shelves stocked. Both."

She unlocks a drawer under the counter and counts it out, coin by coin, a clink for each, and ties it in a scrap of waxed cloth. Her last coin lands harder than the rest.

"His name was Tam. Brown cloth cover, a strap, my mark on the first page. If you find it, do not read it in the dark and do not leave it where the damp can reach it." She pushes the bundle across. "Go and outfit yourself. The road does not wait on a store's troubles."

Beyond the doorway, the light cart is still not back.
