## 2026-10-07 · Miasma Marks (slow-burn Fallout) · `#crows #fallout #marks #built #untested`

## 2026-10-07 · Ruin Boons, after Stonetop arcana · `#stonetop-arcana #boons #village #crows #nimble #parked`
- **Idea:** a boon is a lead from a ruin: card with requirements to unlock (cycle days at the Bookseller C51, a material, a Mind RR "you risk") and a power. Each boon carries its own consequence list; the player picks one to mark on a bad result. In Crows the consequences become marks in pack slots.
- **Stonetop structure (Book I, arcana, p.59-60ff):** minor arcana = card with front (what you see + unlock requirements) and back (the move). Major arcana = benefit up front, mysteries to unlock, a limited consequence list you mark by choice (each once; no un-marking). Find them by Know Things, exploring dangerous places, or putting the word out. All rights reserved: take the structure, write new cards.
- **User line:** "Legends tell of men who sought out ruin for the boons." The whole game in one line.
- **Example:** Saltblood (drowned barrow). Unlock: 2 cycle days, a salt ingot, Mind RR (miss costs a wound). Power: once per DT after a wound, harden blood into a weapon. Consequences: grave breath (NPC reactions), cannot cross running water, something below knows your name (seeds a Threat).
- **Tactical choice (user, good):** take the lead (no value now) or the gem (gc, Prosperity). Both fill a pack slot, and slots are the death clock. Add: a lead can be lost (a wound in its slot, fire, water) and research costs cycle days that compete with trade. `[HOUSE]`
- **Reflection:** this is scope creep for Crows right now (touches village cycle, pack slots, sealed template, before anything is tested). Decision: parked. **User's thought: it may fit a Nimble game better** (Stonetop arcana married to Nimble). I have not seen the Nimble rules `[UNSOURCED]`; the hypothesis is that Nimble's lighter core leaves room for a card-driven unlock-and-consequence layer that Crows' slot economy already uses up. Revisit when a Nimble game exists.

## 2026-10-08 · Narrative-only vs dense mechanics for an LLM Ref (Realis ashcan) · `#mda #lean #tokens #crows #nimble #parked`
- **Source:** Realis, Ashcan Edition (Austin Walker, Possible Worlds Games, 2025, all rights reserved; paraphrase only). No dice. A Sentence is a declarative "I always ..." statement with a rank (+0 to +3). When two Sentences conflict the higher rank prevails; on a tie the Counteractor wins; the loser's Sentence is Countered and unusable for the scene. After a Class Sentence is Countered three times its owner adds a limiting condition and raises its rank by 1 (Realization); a +3 Sentence Countered three times is Retired. Moon Sentences give places and peoples their own. Tokens, Bonds, Dreams exist.
- **Question (user):** would a purely narrative, language-based system help the LLM more than a dense mechanical one?
- **Reflection (hypothesis, untested):** our failures were arithmetic, state keeping, fairness under hidden numbers and judgment calls. A rank comparison removes arithmetic and nearly all state (integers plus a per-scene countered flag), so it suits an LLM. What it does not remove is the judgment "does this Sentence apply here", where an LLM tends to say yes; the tie goes to the Counter rule is a useful brake. The cost is aesthetic: no dice means no own-luck tension, and the user has said the roll in hand matters to him. Realis is about transformation, not attrition, so it does not carry Crows' pack-slot dread.
- **Useful transfer:** Realization is an escalation engine (failure makes a power narrower and stronger) and maps onto marks and arcana consequences. `#fallout #marks #stonetop-arcana`
- **Option:** a Sentence-style layer for non-combat and social beats only (replaces Pull Strings/Sense Motive) while Crows keeps dice for combat, travel and attrition. Needs a test; not started.
- **Decision:** none. Not tested. Revisit with the Nimble game.

## 2026-10-09 · ASCII map, range bands, cover and facing · `#crows #map #lean #parked #decision`
- **Question (user):** implement an ASCII map? Gut: band by band with cover marked; the rub is facing, since attacks from behind would favour monsters.
- **What exists:** the engine already has a grid map (`map`, `area`, `look`, `tok`, `dist`, `los`, `pos`, fog of war, glyph legend). Pilot 02's cellar had no map, so only `pos` was used.
- **Printed Crows rules (R, Combat):** no facing. Flanking: you and an ally on opposite sides of an enemy gain an edge on melee attacks against it (not if prone or grabbed). Cover: at least half the target blocked, attacker takes a bane. Concealment: light is a bane, heavy a double bane. High ground: edge. Ranges and effects are in squares (Melee 1, Ranged 5, push N, speed 5). `[page refs not re-checked this session]`
- **Research (web, standard search):** range bands are common (13th Age engaged/nearby/far, Agon, FFG Star Wars, Traveller zones); they scale well but lose cover and flanking unless zones carry them. LLM evidence: models read ASCII grids better than they write them, so keep the authoritative map in code and pass a rendered view. Sources: Gnome Stew battlemat alternatives, ENWorld abstract combat threads, arXiv 2604.14641 (ASCII and spatial reasoning), a LessWrong note on Claude and ASCII art.
- **Reflection:** bands as a replacement would force a HOUSE conversion of every printed range, spell and push, which is creep. Bands as a rendered view over the grid costs nothing in rules. Facing is a per-token state plus a Ref judgment, and the printed flank rule already covers the "from behind" case symmetrically: whoever holds opposite sides. In solo play the crow rarely has allies, so monsters flank more often. That asymmetry is in the printed game, not added by us.
- **Proposal (not built, needs go):** three engine commands, no rules text change. `scene` prints tokens by band (adjacent, within 5, farther) with cover flags. `cover A B` computes cover from the line between centres and the blocking glyphs. `flank T` reports opposite-side pairs around T. Tools own geometry; the Ref pastes the output. `#ledger #tokens`

## 2026-10-11 · Three LLM-and-games papers: what they change · `#llm #lean #measure #decision #parked`
- **Sources (read: abstracts, methods and results, discussion; the survey only its GM and limitations sections):**
  1. Jørgensen et al., "Static vs. Agentic Game Master AI for Facilitating Solo Role-Playing Experiences" (arXiv 2502.19519, 2025). Two versions of a solo D&D-style GM, n=12.
  2. Chen et al., "Assessing Rule Adherence of LLM Adjudicators in Call of Cthulhu TRPG" (arXiv 2607.02802, Oct 2026). CoC-Seduce benchmark, 22 models, 5,376 samples.
  3. Gallotta et al., "Large Language Models and Games: A Survey and Roadmap" (arXiv 2402.18659, 2024).
- **Paper 2, the sharpest finding:** LLM adjudicators err toward leniency. They resolve about 1 in 10 mandatory-roll scenarios without a roll (mean 9.26%); wrongly demanding a roll is near zero (0.08%). Pseudo-logical framing (a plausible causal story for why the action works) is the strongest lever (17.05% average, up to 43% for one model); authority claims about 9.6%, neutral baseline 3.6%. Newer or reasoning models were not reliably better. Claude Sonnet and Opus models were among the lowest (about 3 to 5%). The adversary was LLM-written player text, zero-shot, no tools, so solo play is a looser fit; the direction (compliance bias) still applies.
- **Paper 1:** v1 (full game state in the prompt each turn) lost coherence: enemy and item counts drifted, early context was forgotten. v2 (Narrator plus Archivist agents with ReAct tool calls) was preferred, 9 of 14 constructs significant, small sample, p near 0.10 on most of the rest. Few-shot examples in prompts and in tool descriptions drove correct tool use, including calling the Battle tool the right number of times. Players felt "guided by a GM" in v2 and "guiding the GM" in v1. Content filters interrupted combat in the API tests.
- **Paper 3:** hallucination is inevitable; LLM GMs are over-compliant and accommodate bizarre requests; external stores (databases, RAG) are the usual consistency fix; long context does not solve long-term state tracking. Mostly confirms the design.
- **Reflection:** our architecture (Ledger and engine as the Archivist) matches paper 1's winning pattern. Paper 2 exposes a leak we wrote ourselves: the "clever plan means no roll" and "no roll if it works 80% of the time" lines let the Ref talk itself out of a roll. The paper's pseudo-logic is the same shape as a player explaining why it works.
- **Candidate changes, ranked:** (A) flip the default: if anything is at stake, roll; a no-roll ruling is stated as `No roll: <why>` and the player's "roll it" is always honoured; (B) `engine round`, one call that runs initiative and every foe action from a roster, fixing under- and over-calling (paper 1's Battle tool problem); (C) one few-shot example per command in the help and one example round in the card; (D) optional scene-end Archivist pass by a separate agent for SAVE and sealed lines (costs tokens); (E) pilot measure: log every no-roll ruling and check each for a stake. None built.

## 2026-10-11 · Why the pilots went stale, and what progress is worth · `#aesthetics #mda #pilot #decision #parked`
- **User diagnosis:** the same chapel two runs in a row got stale. Finding gold quickly raised interest; at the end he wanted to see the crow progress, and Pilot 05 reached the most gc so far. Frame: ethos and logos were missing, the pull for the game's existence (pathos) is strong; the player needs something to be motivated by. Session flow as upbeats, downbeats, contrast, the spark and Ma (the pause or negative space) is what good GMing does; probably too granular to program, but felt.
- **My reading (to confirm):** ethos = the world feels specific and credible (named places and people, a voice); logos = the threads make sense (why this place, what is happening); pathos = something to care about and a reason to push on. The pilot sealed files were "fresh", so no Threat or named person was live, which removes ethos and logos from the first scene.
- **Finding:** progress is already in the printed rules. Crows XP is treasure value (gc), and TXP milestones grant bonuses after a rest: 100, 500, 1,250, 2,250, 3,500, 5,000, 10,000, 20,000, 30,000 for expertise or Stamina, characteristics at 5,000, 15,000, 30,000 (Characters book p.6 to 7, "Expertise & Stamina Advancement"). 13 gc is 13% of the first milestone. Nothing in our tools surfaces it. That is why gold felt good and why the crow felt static.
- **Candidates (none built):** (1) Ledger `xp N`, status shows `TXP t, n to the next bonus`, `rest` announces a milestone and the three printed options; (2) novelty: `site NAME` log, the Ref may not reuse the previous session's site; (3) a `beat up|down|ma` log with one-line advice (after two of the same, the next is the contrast) so beats are three tags, not a theory; (4) one live Threat and one named person active at session zero, from the sealed template; (5) interest tick in the checklist (already proposed).
- **Reflection:** the cheapest and best-sourced is (1), because it turns an existing rule into a visible reward loop. The Ma and contrast ideas fit the card's existing "two quiet turns" and "same consequence twice" Nevers; (3) generalises them.

## 2026-10-15 · Fatherfog (FRPG 0E v0.2) measured as a donor, not a pivot · `#fatherfog #mda #lean #measure #parked`
- **Read:** introduction, character creation steps, How to Play (dice, advantage, stats and saves, Hope and Despair, skills, unnatural abilities, rest and improvement, combat, perils, healing, travel, death), the Guide's section (principles, encounter variety, player requests, two rounds or ten minutes, start of the Despair table, sample enemy format). **Not read:** class details, equipment and legacy artifact tables, the Death Table, most of the bestiary.
- **Core:** all d10s. Three Stats (about 30 average) and three Saves (about 25). Roll under on d100; doubles are criticals; 00 always a critical success, 99 a critical failure. Advantage and disadvantage roll twice. A "Bit" is the ones digit of a d100, so one roll yields success and a magnitude. Default failure: lose 1 Hope. Hope is the stress track; Hope Saves lead to Despair, a d100 table whose results include upsides (Triumph, Rally, Resilient, Inspired). Health equals Strength. Dice are player-facing: the Guide never rolls. NPCs have no turn; their attacks are part of the Guide's description. Rests restore Health and Hope and improve one Stat or Save by 1 per full night; Skill Points come from time played, milestones and keeping watch.
- **Strengths for my purposes:** tiny state (6 numbers plus Hope, Health, flat skill bonuses); no foe rolls or foe roster; roll-under maps to the solo kit's `roll_under` type; Hope and Despair give a built-in pacing loop with upsides (the user's upbeat and downbeat idea); Death's price is the Marks idea at the core; the Guide's section directly addresses staleness (encounter variety types, listen to what players ask for, legacy artifact must pay off, failures never dead-end, combat in 2 rounds or 10 minutes, short sessions); steady visible progress (a stat point per night).

- **Candidate steals (none built):** (1) Bits: print the ones digit on every d100-style result so one roll gives success and magnitude, cutting round trips; (2) encounter-type rotation (social, exploration, puzzle or trap, combat, investigation, moral) tracked by the engine like `beat`, advising a type not used in the last two scenes; (3) legacy artifact: each crow starts with one item the world must pay off, a live-start hook; (4) Despair-style table with upsides for Miasma, so a breakdown can also be a spark; (5) Death's price and Gifts for a successor: progression through death, fits marks; (6) rule "failures never dead-end: success at a cost".
- **Reflection:** a strong donor for pacing and hooks, a weak replacement for a dungeon crawl with tactics. Nothing here suggests changing the Crows base.

## 2026-10-21 · Warlord rules via a video summary (Questing Beast, "Did a card game fix DnD's combat system?") · `#decision #shipping #mda #parked`
- **Source quality:** an AI-made transcript summary of a video, second hand; it is not the rulebook. Treat every rule below as unverified until checked against the user's own copy.
- **Rules as summarised:** each side has a Warlord (back rank), two level-2 units (second rank) and three level-1 units (front rank). Initiative d20, then alternating orders until orders are spent. Orders: spend (tap) for a printed ability, move up a rank, play action cards (class symbols and level limits), equip items (unit level plus its rank number must meet the item level), attack. Formation rule: a rank behind never holds more units than the rank in front; illegal formations make rear units fall forward (spent or stunned if more than one rank). Attack: d20 plus bonus against the target's open Armor Class, front-rank targets, a hit deals 1 damage; most units die at 1, tougher at 2, Warlords at 3. Win by exposing and slaying the Warlord. Round end: discard, redraw to 5, untap.
- **Video's argument:** swinginess is eased by commanding many units, ranged attacks and stacked bonuses; threshold AC is fast; low hit points make every hit change the board; open AC values give players clear tactical choices.
- **Fit to SPEC.md:** `vs_dc` resolution already covers d20 plus bonus against AC with criticals; tap and stun are conditions; hand of 5 and untap are container ops; an automa is a priority table over orders. **New primitive needed:** a `ranks` spatial model (ordered ranks with capacity constraints, fall-forward as an automatic effect, a legality check after every event). Card texts beyond the starter decks stay as rulings.
- **Reflection:** a small, formal game with little hidden state, so a good pressure test and a model for an original rank-and-file skirmish that could ship. Game mechanics such as ranks, tapping and d20 against AC are not the publisher's expression, but names, card text and art are; check licensing before using any of those (I am not giving legal advice).
