Every row below is generated from the rulebook extract the macros themselves compute from, not written by hand, so this page and the macros it describes can never quietly disagree. For each entry: what the macro computes, in plain language; the book chapter and page it comes from; and the exact sentence, quoted verbatim, that the macro is implementing. Page numbers are pulled from the book's own printed page markers in the source text, not typed in by hand, so a wrong page number here would mean the lookup itself is broken, not a typo.

15A skill roll is percentile dice (tens die + ones die) read as a number 1-100, compared to the skill value. Equal-to-or-under succeeds.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack (shared roll code in _lib.js)“If the roll is over the skill value, it fails. 16A skill's Score is the tens digit of its value, used only where a rule says so, and never changed by modifiers.TBE: Skill Roll (skills over 100), TBE: Character Wizard“A skill also has a Score (the tens digit of a skill value). The Score is used only in specific cases, indicated when relevant, and is never altered by modifiers.”
18On a success, the tens die shown is the number of Success Levels (SL). A 0 on the tens die counts as 1 SL, not 10 -- this is a different rule from Weave Mastery's 0-counts-as-10.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“When you roll your skill value or under, you succeed, and the number showing on the tens die is the number of Success Levels (SLs). The Success Level represents the quality of success and, when relevant to the outcome, can have various effects based on the particular skill. On any successful skill roll, a 0 on the tens die counts as 1 SL.”
18Doubles under your skill, or rolling the skill value exactly, is a critical success: +3 SL.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“A critical success is an extraordinary skill roll result, and is achieved when you roll doubles under your skill, or when you roll your skill value exactly. A critical success adds +3 Success Levels to your roll.”
18Doubles over your skill value is a critical failure.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“A critical failure, on the other hand, can carry extra consequences. Rolling doubles over your skill value is a critical failure.”
1801-05 always succeeds (even at skill 0 or below, which still succeeds on 01-04 and crits on 05); 99-00 always fails.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“A roll of 01-05 is always a success, regardless of skill value. Consequently, if your skill value is ever modified to zero or below, it still succeeds on a roll of 01-04, and critically succeeds on a 05. A roll of 99-00 is always a failure (and if over your skill, a critical failure), regardless of skill value.”
18At skill 99 or 100, rolling the exact value is a failure, not a critical success.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“In the rare case you have a skill value of 99 or 100, rolling your skill value exactly is still a failure, not a critical success.”
18A modified skill of 100+ can never critically fail; on a success it adds bonus SL equal to the tens digit of (skill - 100), minimum 1.TBE: Skill Roll, TBE: Opposed Roll, TBE: Attack“These skills still fail on a roll of 99-00, but can no longer critically fail. If a skill ever has (or is modified to) a value over 100, roll it normally, but a success will benefit from extra SLs. To determine how many, subtract 100 from the skill value. The skill's resulting tens die value (the Score) acts as extra SLs (with a minimum of 1) on a successful skill roll.”
19A standard (unopposed) roll needs only 1 SL to succeed; extra SLs only matter when the specific skill or situation says so.TBE: Skill Roll“A standard roll requires only 1 SL to succeed. However, additional Success Levels can sometimes produce a better outcome.”
19Opposed rolls: whoever earns more SL wins (0 SL can still win). Tie-breaks in order: higher die roll, then higher modified skill, then critical beats non-critical; a normal failure beats a critical failure.TBE: Opposed Roll“Both sides roll their skills and whoever earns more SLs is the winner. It's possible to win an opposed roll with 0 SLs”

86Death Threshold starts at 20 and rises 2 per Attribute point spent on it -- the ceiling on total lethal Wound Points before death.TBE: Character Wizard, the character sheet“Death Threshold (DT) is the maximum number of total lethal Wound Points you can suffer in all combined hit locations before you are killed. A Starting Death Threshold is 20. A Increase the Death Threshold by 2 per Attribute point spent.”
87Lethality Level = 1/3 of Death Threshold, rounded up. A A character's Lethality Level is equal to 1/3 of their Death Threshold, rounded up. A If they drop in Shock and their total lethal Wound Points exceed their Lethality Level, they are now Dying.”
