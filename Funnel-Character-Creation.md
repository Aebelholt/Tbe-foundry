# Starting as a Funnel: Character Creation the Hard Way

Most games start with you rolling one hero and giving them a history. This
one starts differently: you roll four ordinary people from the same town,
and you play all four. What becomes of them, and which one (if any) ends
up your character going forward, you'll find out at the table.

## Building your four

Nobody you roll is a warrior, a wizard, or a rogue. They're a tanner, a
midwife, a gate guard, a poacher, a scribe, whatever the dice hand you.
Each one comes with:

- **A trade**, and the two or three skills that trade actually uses,
  raised above everyone else's starting level. A blacksmith is better at
  the forge than at anything else, and it shows on the sheet.
- **Two Ability Scores**, rolled the same way the book rolls them for any
  character. These aren't flaws, they're specific things this person is
  unusually good or bad at, and they can come with a Talent or a trait
  worth knowing about.
- **What they're carrying, what they know about the town, and what
  they'd risk their life for.** A carter knows the three roads out and
  which season closes each. A hedge-witch knows her reputation will get
  her blamed by dawn. That last part, what they'd risk it for, matters:
  hold onto it.
- **A Bond.** One person, usually in someone else's group of four, that
  this townsperson would break formation for. You'll likely end up
  wanting your people and someone else's people in the same place at the
  same time, and this is why.

Everything else about them, the skills their trade doesn't touch, sits at
the ordinary starting value. They're not built for danger. Keep that in
mind.

## Play them

From here, play your four the way you'd play any character: react to
what the session throws at you, in character, one decision at a time.
Whoever's keeping score will let you know what's happened to each of
your people as it happens; that record follows them.

## Coming out the other side

At some point, the GM will ask who's left standing, and one of your
survivors gets to become your real character going forward. Nothing about
who they were is thrown away: their trade skills, their Ability Score
gifts, everything carries over. Two things get added on top, and only
because of what they actually went through:

- **One skill goes up**, whatever the moment taught them, sharp and
  sudden.
- **They get a goal**, pulled straight out of what happened to them.
  It isn't assigned, it's earned.

From there they go through the normal process for the parts this method
skips: a career, a Cultural Background, Life Events. Those are about who
this person was before you knew them, and you get to answer that only
now, once you already know who they had to become to get this far.

## Why bother

A rolled hero built cold is a good character on paper. A character who
came out of this is a good character because you watched them become
one. Nobody handed you their backstory on a table, you earned it playing
them.
